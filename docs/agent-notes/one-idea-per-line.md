# One idea per line when contrasting

*When a sentence sets two different concepts against each other, split it: one concept per sentence, each sentence on its own line*

On the Ch 6 KL slide (2026-10-26) the keypoint read "This lecture uses both directions: fitting a model averages over the data — cover the data; keeping a search near the generator averages over the search — stay where the generator lives." The professor said a contrast written as one continuous run does not read as a contrast. Each concept belongs in its own sentence, and a new concept starts a new line.

**Why:** the professor lectures from the slide. Two ideas joined by a semicolon blur into one, and the eye cannot find where the second begins. On separate lines, the parallel is visible before it is read.

**How to apply:** in a `::: keypoint`, put each contrasted concept on its own source line. The engine renders every source line as its own line (`build.mjs` wraps them in `<span class="tl">`, and `deck.css` adds a 6px gap). A one-line keypoint renders exactly as before. Split when the sentence opposes two *different* things: two directions, two methods, two terms of a loss, this lecture and an earlier one, what one tool repairs and what another repairs. Keep a claim and its own limit together ("A generator structures the search; it does not create evidence") — that is one idea. Lists of three or more parts that the slide asks to keep apart get one line each too (Ch 6 "Learning route"). Every added line costs about 35px, so run the visual pass. If a slide starts shrinking (`data-ts`), move an agenda or forward-pointer sentence to the presenter note rather than squeezing the contrast back into one line. Splitting is necessary, not sufficient. On Ch 6 "Where we are" the three-line split still did not show its message at a glance (professor, same day). What worked was this. Make the lines parallel and short, mirroring the title's own contrast: "Lecture 5 **judges** a design: …" / "Lecture 6 **proposes** designs: …". Highlight a short phrase that states the point ("==Proposing is not judging==") rather than a whole clause. Trim table cells until each fits on one line. Applied to Ch 6 first, then Ch 5 (18 keypoints, 2026-10-26). The other chapters still carry about ninety semicolon-joined keypoints to review.

### 1.3 The VAE, pp. 11–14 → **Act 3**

| p | act | content |
|---|---|---|
| **11** | **A3** | **Variational Autoencoders.** *One of the most widely used generative models; consists of a probabilistic encoder and decoder network.* **Generative process:** sample `z ~ N(0,I)`, generate `x = p_θ(x|z)`. **Inference process:** variational inference. Optimal approximate posterior:<br>`q_{φ*}(z|x) = argmax_φ E_{z~q}[log p_θ(X,Z)/q_φ(z|x)] = argmax_φ E_{q_φ}[log p_θ(x|z)] − KL[q_φ(z|x)‖p_θ(z)]`<br>**Figure (top right):** the MNIST 2-D latent scatter, ten digit classes in ten colours forming a ragged pinwheel around the origin; beside it the 20×20 grid of digits decoded on a lattice of `z` (the digits morph continuously between classes); below it the `p(Z)` bivariate-Gaussian surface. **This figure is the source of the "walk the latent space" widget.** |
| **12** | **A3** | **VAE — the reparameterisation trick.** `q_φ(z|x) = N(μ_φ(x), σ_φ(x))`, `p_θ(x|z) = N(μ_θ(z), σ_θ(z))`. **Figure:** a face `x_i` → MLP → two heads `μ_φ(x)`, `σ_φ(x)` → `μ_φ(x) + ε σ_φ(x)` with `ε ~ N(0,I)` fed in from below → MLP → reconstructed face `p_θ(x|z)`. Objective in its concrete, implementable form:<br>`max_{φ,θ} (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^N log p_θ(x_i | μ_φ(x) + ε σ_φ(x)) − D_KL[N(μ_φ(x_i), σ_φ(x_i)) ‖ N(0,I)]` |
| **13** | **A3** | **VAE — reading the two terms.** `max_{φ,θ} = E_{z~q_φ(z|x)}[log p_θ(x|z)]` (**reconstruction**) `− D_KL(q_φ(z|x)‖p(z))` (**KL regularization**). **Three-panel figure**, the best explanatory figure in the deck:<br>① *"Penalizing reconstruction loss encourages the distribution to describe the input"* — a blue bump displaced right of a grey prior bump, arrow pushing right: *our distribution deviates from the prior to describe some characteristic of the data*;<br>② *"Without regularization, our network can 'cheat' by learning narrow distributions"* — a needle-thin spike: *with a small enough variance, this distribution is effectively only representing a single value*;<br>③ *"Penalizing KL divergence acts as a regularizing force"* — arrows pulling the bump back toward zero mean and widening it: *attract distribution to have zero mean* / *ensure sufficient variance to yield a smooth latent space*.<br>Caption line: ***"The VAE objective arranges data on a compact manifold (we can sample from) in a continuous smooth way."*** ← the claim the β-dial widget must earn. |
| **14** | **A3→A4** | **Conditional Variational Auto Encoder — the bridge slide the tex drops.** `q_φ(z|x,y) = N(μ_φ(x,y), σ_φ(x,y))`, `p_θ(x|z,y) = N(μ_θ(z,y), σ_θ(z,y))`. Graphical model: `Y → X`, `Y → Z`, `Z → X` solid; dashed inference arrows `X ⇢ Z`, `Y ⇢ Z`. Objective:<br>`max_{θ,φ} E_{z~q_φ(z|x,y)}[log p_θ(x|z,**y**)] − D_KL(q_φ(z|x,**y**) ‖ p_θ(z|**y**))`<br>Figure: SVHN samples, a 10×N grid, rows 0–9 — the *class* is fixed along a row and the *style* varies along it. ### 1.5 Diffusion — the energy/score view, pp. 23–37 → **appendix + one act slide**

| p | act | content |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | X | **EBM perspective.** `p_θ(x) = exp(−ε_θ(x))/Z_θ`, `Z_θ = ∫ exp(−ε_θ(x))dx`. Three ways to formulate an objective: ① maximum likelihood, ② score matching (score-based generative model), ③ noise-contrastive estimation. |
| 24 | X | ① **Maximum likelihood.** `∇_θ log p_θ(x) = −∇_θ ε_θ(x) − ` ==`∇_θ log Z_θ`== (red). `Z_θ` is intractable for a general energy, so likelihood-based models must either **restrict the architecture** (causal convolutions in autoregressive models, invertible networks in flows) to make `Z_θ` tractable, or **approximate the normalising constant** (variational inference in VAEs, MCMC in contrastive divergence). ← the cleanest statement in the deck of *why* the VAE exists. |
| **25** | **X** | ② **Score matching.** Define the score `∇_x log p(x)`. Then `s_θ(x) ≈ ∇_x log p(x) = ∇_x log(exp(−ε_θ)/Z_θ) = −∇_x ε_θ(x) − ` ~~`∇_x log Z_θ`~~ `= −∇_x ε_θ(x)` — **the normalising constant differentiates away**, because `Z_θ` does not depend on `x`. Objective = Fisher divergence `D_F(p_data, p_θ) = E_{p_data}[½‖∇_x log p_data − s_θ(x)‖²]`. |
| 26 | X | Training objective `½E_{p_data}[‖∇log p_data − s_θ‖²]` is not directly computable (the data score is unknown); equivalent forms: **Hyvärinen 2005** `E_{p_data}[tr(∇_x s_θ(x)) + ½‖s_θ(x)‖²]`; **Vincent 2011** denoising score matching `½E_{q_σ(x̃|x)p_data(x)}[‖∇_x̃ log q_σ(x̃|x) − s_θ(x̃)‖²]`. |
| 27 | X | **Langevin dynamics.** `x̃_t = x̃_{t−1} + (ε/2)∇_x log p(x̃_{t−1}) + √ε z_t`, `z_t ~ N(0,I)`; as `ε→0, T→∞` the law of `x̃_T` is `p(x)` (Welling & Teh 2011). | **28** | **A3 / C** | **"Naive score-based generative modeling and its pitfalls" — the slide that rhymes with Lecture 5.** *"The estimated score functions are inaccurate in low density regions, where few data points are available for computing the score matching objective."*<br>`E_{p(x)}[‖∇_x log p(x) − s_θ(x)‖²] = ∫ p(x)‖∇_x log p(x) − s_θ(x)‖² dx`<br>*"Since the ℓ₂ differences are **weighted by p(x)**, they are largely ignored in low density regions where p(x) is small."*<br>**Three-panel figure:** *Data density* (two orange Gaussian blobs, one strong top-right, one faint bottom-left, on a near-white field); *Data scores* (a vector field with two cyan dashed "**Accurate**" boxes over the two blobs and a large pink "**Inaccurate**" region covering everything between); *Estimated scores* (the same, with the arrows in the pink region visibly wrong). **This is precisely Lecture 5's "the model is unconstrained where there is no evidence", arriving inside the generative half.** |
| 29–30 | X | *(the same slide twice)* **Multiple noise perturbations.** Solution: *perturb data points with noise and train score-based models on the noisy data instead* — the same three panels, now all "Accurate". *"When the noise magnitude is sufficiently large, it can populate low data density regions to improve the accuracy of estimated scores."* Choosing the scale: `p_{σ_i}(x) = ∫ p(y) N(x; y, σ_i²I) dy`; noise-conditional score model `s_θ(x,i) ≈ ∇_x log p_{σ_i}(x)`; objective `Σ_{i=1}^L λ(i) E_{p_{σ_i}}[‖∇log p_{σ_i} − s_θ(x,i)‖²]` with `λ(i) = σ_i²`; parameterise `s_θ(x,i)` with U-Net skip connections. Figure: three columns `σ₁ < σ₂ < σ₃`, the blobs progressively blurring and the field progressively filling. |
| 31 | X | **Annealed Langevin dynamics** — run Langevin for `i = L, L−1, …, 1` in sequence; the noise scale *anneals* down. Figure: three vector fields for σ₁,σ₂,σ₃ plus two grids of intermediate noisy samples. |
| 32 | X | **Unification with SDE.** Two approaches so far: DDPM (ELBO objective) and NCSN (score-matching objective). | 33 | X | **Perturbing data with an SDE.** As the number of noise scales → ∞, the perturbation is a continuous-time stochastic process: `dx = f(x,t)dt + g(t)dw`, with `f` the drift, `g` the diffusion coefficient, `w` Brownian motion. `{x(t)}_{t∈[0,T]}` the solution, `p_t(x)` the marginal, `p₀(x)` the data. |
| 34 | X | **Reversing the SDE.** `dx = [f(x,t) − g²(t)∇_x log p_t(x)]dt + g(t)dw̄`, solved backwards from `t = T` to `t = 0`; `dt` a negative infinitesimal step. Figure: the noise field collapsing back into the two-branch density. |
| 35 | X | **Estimating the reverse SDE.** Need `p_T(x)` and the score `∇_x log p_t(x)`; train a **time-dependent** score model `s_θ(x,t) ≈ ∇_x log p_t(x)` (analogue of the noise-conditional model) with `E_{t~U(0,T)}E_{p_t(x)}[λ(t)‖∇log p_t − s_θ(x,t)‖²]`. Figure: the forward SDE (dog → noise) above the reverse SDE (noise → dog) with the score function boxed in the reverse drift. |
| 36 | X | **Which SDE is which.** NCSN = **variance exploding**: `dx = √(d[σ²(t)]/dt) dw`. DDPM = **variance preserving**: `dx = −½β(t)x dt + √β(t) dw`. Plus a new *sub-VP* SDE `dx = −½β(t)x dt + √(β(t)(1 − e^{−2∫β}))dw`. |
| 37 | X | **Summary table.** VAE→**DDPM** objective `E_t E_{x₀} E_q[D_KL(q(x_{t−1}|x_t,x₀), p(x_{t−1}|x_t))]`, sampled by repeatedly running the trained generator. ## 3 · What to build from this

**Slides the tex has no frame for and that must be restored:**

1. **P1 p.4 (c)** — the three named advantages of sampling over searching (no optimisation to solve; diversity by multi-modality; constraints satisfied implicitly). This is the chapter's *why*, and the tex only has the first.
2. **P1 p.4 (b)** — the criticism of Lecture 5: *conservatism limits the expressivity of the model and often fails to find a better design.* The pair Ch 5 ↔ Ch 6 is not "one is right"; each buys its safety with a different cost.
3. **P1 p.14 — the CVAE.** The tex introduces conditioning only in Act 4 via MINs; the source builds the inverse map *inside the VAE act* and says so: "y can be the desired property, x the corresponding design". This makes Act 3 → Act 4 a single step rather than a jump.
4. **P1 p.28 — the score's low-density blind spot.** `E_p(x)[‖∇log p − s_θ‖²]` is *weighted by p(x)*, so the estimate is unconstrained exactly where there is no data. This is Lecture 5's thesis restated inside the generative half, and the fix (noise perturbation to populate low-density regions) is the mirror image of conservatism.
5. **P1 p.39 — `∇_x log p(x|y) = ∇_x log p(x) + ∇_x log p(y|x)`.** The whole lecture on one line: prior over designs + forward oracle = the conditional you sample.
6. **P2 pp. 10–16 — CbAS in full.** The rare-event failure of the naive estimator, and the relaxed-condition ladder that fixes it. The tex mentions CbAS nowhere; Ch 5's benchmark table already cites it.
7. **P2 pp. 22–24 — MINs' reweighting and Approx-Infer.** The tex's Act 4 has the inverse map and the query but not *which y to train on* or *how far past the data to ask*, which is where the whole extrapolation hazard lives.
8. **P2 pp. 25–27 — the MIN experiment numbers**, especially the word **"Invalid"** in the *Forward map* column. Lecture 5's failure, reported as a table entry.
9. **P2 pp. 30–41 — DDOM and BootGen**, including the fact that BootGen answers the generative half's own OOD failure with **an ensemble and a proxy re-ranker** — i.e. it marries Ch 5 and Ch 6 in one algorithm.

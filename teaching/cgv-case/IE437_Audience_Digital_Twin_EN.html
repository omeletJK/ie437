04A Limits of movie-level regression

Only a few hundred movies are released nationwide each year.
Regression struggles to ensure accuracy and reliable uncertainty estimates

Training samples for movie-level regression
300~600 movies / year
Annual nationwide releases; dozens of features such as genre, cast, director and IP
VS
Bottom-up viewer × movie observations
400,000+observations
Interactions across Cinema members and movies in their viewing histories
  • Hundreds of samples + dozens of features → risk of overfitting and unstable extrapolation to new combinations such as a new director and new IP
  • With few samples, prediction intervals are themselves unstable; even the appropriate interval width is hard to estimate reliably
  • One or two outliers, such as a blockbuster or major flop, can distort all regression coefficients.
Replacing movie counts with member × movie observations enables much more stable statistical estimates within the same market.

    11 · MOVIE REPRESENTATION SPACE
    11ATemporal Point Process

    A person's viewing history is a sequence of events over time.
    History creates intensity λ, which determines the next movie choice

    SF Action Drama λ_A New movie A (SF) λ_B New movie B (romance) ◎ = this person's actual viewing event
    ① WHEN / HOW OFTEN · VISIT FREQUENCY
    aᵤ + rᵤ(gap) = min(1, gap / recovery daysᵤ)

    Annual propensity fᵤ enters as intercept aᵤ. Recovery term r suppresses an immediate return. Ten visits a year is an expectation, not a quota. Attractive releases can shorten the interval.

    ② WHAT · CONTENT FIT + HISTORY STATE
    λᵤₘ = softplus(aᵤ + sᵤₘ + g(Hᵤ, m)) · kₘ(d) · rᵤ(gap)

    sᵤₘ measures taste–content fit; g(Hᵤ,m) = Σᵥ Gₘᵥ·zᵤᵥ(t) captures history propagated from recently watched similar movie nodes. zᵤᵥ decays between events and is updated by a GRU on a visit. The effect can reinforce taste or suppress intensity through fatigue.

    ③ NEXT VIEWING EVENT · TIME AND MOVIE TOGETHER
    Λ(t) = Σₘ λₘ(t | Hₜ),  S(t) = exp(−∫τt Λ)
    P(next = m, t ∈ [a,b)) = ∫ab S(t) · λₘ(t) dt

    “When” is the survival function S(t); “what” is the instantaneous share λₘ/Λ. They form one distribution. With daily constant intensity, this becomes S18A's (1−e −L )·λₘ/L.

    M0 (baseline) starts with frequency and intervals as the history term in ②, keeping it interpretable without learning. M1 (research model) extends it with graph history state g(Hᵤ,m). Adopt only if it beats M0 on a real holdout (A12). The method follows KDD 2023 research learning multivariate Hawkes processes with graph recurrent neural networks.

        18A Attendance opportunity model

        Preference and attendance share one joint distribution

        Desire for each movie Independent of dates and showtimes
        →
        Accounts for the theatrical run, competing movies and visit intervals
        →
        TODAY'S JOINT PROBABILITIES
        Choose movie m Compete with movies showing now → choose theater / showtime → assign seat → update last visit
        No Movie Determined in the same distribution, with no second draw; defer to a future opportunity
        L(today) = Σₘ λₘ(today) : total intensity across movies showing today. λₘ is the history-dependent intensity λᵤₘ(d | Hᵤ) in S11A
        P(No Movie) = exp(−L)    P(movie m) = (1−exp(−L)) × λₘ/L
        Attendance and movie choice come from one joint distribution using the same intensities λ, rather than two independently adjusted models or double multiplication of probabilities. Ten visits a year is an expectation, not a fixed quota.
        Attractive new releases shorten visit intervals; a weak lineup delays the next visit. The Twin is not forced to choose a movie every week.

        A12 Movie choice methodology (M0 → M1)

        Implement the interpretable baseline M0 first;
        adopt graph model M1 only after validation

        M0 · BASELINE

        Static content fit + frequency intercept

        Convert taste–content fit and individual frequency intercepts to softplus intensity. Interpretable without learning; implement first.

        M1 · RESEARCH MODEL

        Content graph + individual history state

        Place movies in genre, style and IP clusters. Propagate recent viewing history through a similarity graph. Watching a similar movie may raise or lower intensity through persistent taste, fatigue or budget depletion.

        Adoption gate: M1 must beat both M0 and a learned model without a graph on a real holdout. Otherwise retain M0. Attention identifies referenced connections; verify contribution by removing evidence and recalculating. Do not present attention scores as causal explanations.
        Method reference: Learning Multivariate Hawkes Process via Graph Recurrent Neural Network. KDD 2023: production validation on 20.7M financial-services transactions from 4M people. Explainability limitations follow Attention is not Explanation, NAACL 2019.

        A13Technical Formulation

        Equations for WANT → CAN → CHOOSE → AGGREGATE

        Phase 1 · WANT → daily choice → aggregate (schedule-independent)
        λᵤₘ(d | Hᵤ) = softplus(aᵤ + sᵤₘ + g(Hᵤ, m)) · kₘ(d) · rᵤ(gap)
        History-dependent intensity (events/day): aᵤ frequency intercept; sᵤₘ content fit; g history effect (M0: frequency / intervals; M1: graph state Σᵥ Gₘᵥ zᵤᵥ(t)); kₘ release kernel; r post-visit recovery. BaseMovieDesire(u,m) ≡ log λᵤₘ.
        Pᵤ,d(m) = (1 − e−Lᵤ(d)) · λᵤₘ / Lᵤ(d),  Pᵤ,d(none) = e−Lᵤ(d),  L = Σₘ λᵤₘ
        One daily joint distribution, without separately multiplying visit and movie choice probabilities. In continuous time: S(t)=exp(−∫Λ), P(next=m, t∈[a,b))=∫S(t)λₘ(t)dt (S11A).
        D(m, W) = Σᵤ wᵤ · Pᵤ(m ∈ W),  D(m, d) = Σᵤ wᵤ · Pᵤ,d(m)
        Pᵤ(m∈W) averages simulated paths of the joint distribution over W. A sum of λ is not attendance. wᵤ is the member-to-market calibration weight.
        Phase 2 · CAN → CHOOSE (schedule S feeds back into movie choice)
        ShowtimeUtility(u, m, j | S) = θ · [distance, time fit, format, price, seat availability]
        Accessibility(u, m, S) = logsumexpj ∈ S(m) ShowtimeUtility(u, m, j)
        Measures whether movie m has suitable showtimes in the current schedule. Group equivalent showtimes by equivalence key to increase seat capacity without inflating choice probabilities.
        RealizedUtility(u, m, S) = BaseMovieDesire(u, m)
        + β · Accessibility(u, m, S)
        P(m | S) = eRU(m) / (eU₀ + Σₘ′ eRU(m′)),  P(j | m, S) = softmaxj ShowtimeUtility
        Includes outside option U₀. Equivalent expression: λ observed = λᵤₘ · exp(β·Accessibility). Accessibility enters movie-level probability; choice is not a one-way movie-then-showtime process. Nested calculations feed inclusive value back upward.
        Potential demand and schedule accessibility A may not be fully identifiable from observations alone. Fix travel, price and availability assumptions first; report supplyAdjustedAssumption. Compare M0 (interpretable baseline) and M1 (graph history) under the same evaluation contract (A12).

        Q&A Questions and answers

        Six points to return to

        • The cinema chain needs annual, 1–2 month and 2-week forecasts. The same Audience Twin updates as new information arrives.
        • WANT: people want different movies. Predict individual movie desire from viewing history and taste.
        • CAN: desire does not guarantee attendance. Theater, time, format and distance affect access.
        • CHOOSE → AGGREGATE: a new schedule changes the same person's choices. Summing those choices gives attendance.
        • OPTIMIZE: test several schedules through the Digital Twin to find the best candidate. Validate alongside existing programming.

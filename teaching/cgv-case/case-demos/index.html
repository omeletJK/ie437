From incomplete purchase records
to cinema programming decisions

Two approaches to the same decision: model the response to a schedule directly, or simulate choices in a synthetic audience. Define the decision and its evidence before choosing the model or search algorithm.

01 · RESPONSE MODEL

Schedule → expected response → search

Evaluate feasible schedules with an action-dependent response function. Ask how showtime, capacity and competing films affect purchased tickets.

Executed demo: a synthetic formula evaluator and deterministic iterated local search, ILS. The displayed improvement is a result of this toy calculation.

Assumed audience → choices → aggregation

Evidence boundary. These are selected strategy cases with fictional calculations. The reference demos retain illustrative attendance/visit scores; they do not implement or validate the purchased-ticket target in the HW1 companion. They do not establish learned individual preferences, causal effects, a globally optimal schedule or real business uplift. Neither demo implements a Bayesian optimization posterior/acquisition/update loop.

Begin with the six teaching fields

title_tag · venue_tag · play_day · curtain_time · account_tag · passes_bought

One record is one completed purchase for one screening. The date and time describe the screening. An account buying several passes does not identify every attendee or their preferences. Purchases also do not reveal unsold screenings, screen capacity, decision-time availability or the effects of an alternative schedule.

Ask the class to distinguish observed facts, assumptions and unknowns before showing either approach.

The modeling choice and the search choice

QuestionResponse approachSynthetic audience approach
What evaluates a schedule?A context/action response function. A learned version needs observational coverage and identification checks; this demo uses an explicit toy formula.An assumed population and choice/event simulation. Purchase-account histories alone do not establish actual individual histories.
What chooses a schedule?Enumeration, local search, mathematical optimization or BO, selected separately from the evaluation model. Four cheap candidates can simply be enumerated.
What is the decision boundary?A fixed plan chosen once can be Flat / Stateless / Open-loop. A simulator's internal history does not establish a Stateful or Feedback scheduling decision. Replanning using later observations and commitments requires its own boundary and transition definition.
What must be held common?Feasible schedules, information cutoff, outcome definition, comparison baseline and evaluation conditions. Do not compare the numeric results of these two different demonstration fixtures as relative model performance.

Reproducible HW1 case checks

Open HW1 case checks in either demo. This is a separate, transparent feasibility component: available slots = screens × slots per screen. Its fixed, non-overlapping slots are an explicit teaching assumption.

Normal2 × 3 = 6 slots

Require 4 screenings: feasible, with 2 slots spare.

Boundary2 × 3 = 6 slots

Require 6 screenings: feasible exactly at capacity.

Failure2 × 3 = 6 slots

Require 7 screenings: infeasible. Explain the shortage and revise the inputs.

These checks verify this component, rather than validating the full response, choice or optimization system.

A suggested classroom sequence

  1. Interpret the client brief. State the user, decision, outcome and planning boundary.
  2. Challenge the records. Separate purchasing accounts, tickets and actual attendees; list critical missing inputs.
  3. Run the response demonstration. Change response conditions, run the executed ILS search, and inspect its baseline, calls and constraint violations.
  4. Run the audience demonstration. Step through daily draws and reset the seed. Discuss what the simulated history represents.
  5. Separate model from algorithm. Compare evaluation mechanisms. Explain where BO could be used and why the current demos are not BO.
  6. Run the three case checks. Reproduce normal, boundary and infeasible inputs. Ask what happens when evidence or a feasibility check fails.
  7. Close with validation and adoption. Define a common baseline, a leakage-safe backtest, calibration/sensitivity checks and a small field trial with proceed/revise/stop criteria.
